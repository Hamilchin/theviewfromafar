Model-based RL Project

Vague ideas/questions

  • How can we create policies that are better at exploration and learning the dynamics of an environment? (things like learning from play, for example)

    • A naive idea: train a policy to optimize for "amount of learning" of the world model - perhaps reward actions that lead to large changes in world model weights/high error?

  • The advantage of learning a world models is that they can give you some generalization across goals. To perform a new goal you don't have to relearn your entire model, unlike model-free.

    • Is there a way to get efficient generalization across policies using world models? The immediate thing I can think of is search over next states; no need to retrain a policy there.
    • Is there a way to exploit this advantage, and show that it is better? Current transformer world models like IRIS and TWISTER only show advantage on single atari games.
    • Is transformer model-based stuff better for long horizon/long context tasks?
    • What if we tried model-based RL for goal-conditioned tasks?

  • Can we use world model latents to get better memory-conditioned imitation learning, like Abishek was talking about in class? (non markovian environments with latents, Pranav might be working on something similar)

  • How can we ensure that the latent features learned by the world models are relevant to the task? How to mitigate JEPA collapse?

  • How can we incorporate meta-learning / hierarchical learning / metaRL? Raj Rao side

    • (perhaps for few-shot transfer across environments?)

  • What advantage do models have over simply training PPO in simulation? Seems like an "extra step" to train PPO in imagination, like dreamer does.

    • Being able to search is an advantage but is it better than simply training model-free?
